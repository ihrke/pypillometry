TimescaleModel#

class pypillometry.TimescaleModel(data, eyes=[], binocular=False, timescales=(3000.0, 10000.0, 30000.0, 100000.0, 300000.0), segments=None, fs=20.0, margin=(100, 200), policy='default', variable='pupil', nu=1.5, keep_data=True, name=None)[source]#

Bases: object

Model of the slow and fast fluctuations of the pupil size of one recording (experimental).

The log pupil size is described as a mean per segment, one fast component (Matérn-3/2 with free standard deviation and length scale), a bank of slow components (“atoms”) at fixed time scales whose variances are estimated, and white noise. The model answers how much the pupil varies at each time scale, returns the slow part of the signal (with uncertainty) and reconstructs the signal where it is missing.

The constructor prepares the data and keeps the bookkeeping that is needed to get back to the original samples:

  1. the samples that are unusable under the mask policy (blinks have to be detected before, see d.pupil.blinks_detect()) are extended by margin, so that no part of a blink transient stays in the data;

  2. the signal is reduced to about fs Hz by means of the valid samples in consecutive blocks (block_mean(); nothing is interpolated or filtered);

  3. it is converted to log-%, \(100 \log(\text{pupil} / \text{median})\), which makes the variability independent of the size of the pupil and of its units;

  4. it is split into segments with a mean each: the periods that are not excluded (see exclude_intervals()), or segments.

Parameters:

  • data (EyeData) – the recording, with blinks and artifacts masked

  • eyes (str or list) – eyes to model; if empty, all eyes with pupil data

  • binocular (bool) – if False (default), each eye gets its own model; if True, one model for both eyes: the fast component and the atoms are common to the eyes (with a gain between the eyes, which can differ in amplitude), each eye has its own mean and noise, and one more component describes the difference between the eyes

  • timescales (sequence of float, str or Quantity) – time scales of the atoms (plain numbers are ms); default 3, 10, 30, 100 and 300 s

  • segments (Intervals or list of tuple, optional) – independent stretches of the recording with a mean each (e.g., blocks with different luminance), as for exclude_intervals(); data outside of them are not used. Default: the periods that are not excluded (get_included()).

  • fs (float) – sampling rate of the model in Hz (default 20); the rate that is used is the sampling rate of the data divided by a whole number

  • margin (tuple of float, str or Quantity) – time before and after each run of unusable samples that is not used either (plain numbers are ms; default (100, 200))

  • policy (str, MaskPolicy or list of str) – mask policy that decides which samples are usable (see mask()); default "default"

  • variable (str) – variable to model (default "pupil")

  • nu (float) – smoothness of the atoms (default 1.5)

  • keep_data (bool) – keep a reference to data (default), so that interpolate() and store() work without an argument; the model itself only needs the reduced data

  • name (str, optional) – name of the model (default: the name of data)

Examples

>>> import pypillometry as pp
>>> d = pp.get_example_data("rlmw_002").pupil.blinks_detect()
>>> m = pp.TimescaleModel(d, eyes="left").fit()
>>> m.summary()
>>> m.plot.decomposition()
>>> d = m.interpolate()

fit(prior='medium', hessian=True, maxiter=400, n_jobs=None, progress=None)[source]#

Estimate the parameters of the model.

Maximises the restricted likelihood of the parameters (Kalman filter; the means of the segments are integrated out) plus the log density of the prior.

Parameters:

  • prior (str, GaussianPrior or None) –

    • "weak", "medium" (default) or "strong": the general prior, which describes how the parameters vary between recordings of several experiments (see general_prior())

    • a GaussianPrior, e.g. group.prior of a TimescaleGroup

    • None: maximum likelihood. Atoms without variance then stay at zero, and the split of the variance between neighbouring atoms follows the noise of the recording.

  • hessian (bool) – compute the Hessian at the estimate (for standard errors); default True

  • maxiter (int) – maximum number of iterations of the optimiser

  • n_jobs (int or None) – number of threads for the likelihood evaluations of the optimiser and the Hessian (at most one per parameter); None (default): all cores but one

  • progress (bool or None) – show a progress bar; None (default): if the logging level is “INFO” or “DEBUG”

Return type:

TimescaleModel

Returns:

the model itself (fitted)

forecast_calibration(horizons=('50 ms', '200 ms', '1 s', '5 s', '20 s', '60 s'), step='1 s')[source]#

Accuracy and calibration of forecasts over several horizons.

From the state given the data up to a time point, the model predicts the signal horizons later. A model that describes the dependence over a horizon gives standardised errors with a standard deviation of 1 (below 1: it expects more variability than there is, above 1: less).

Parameters:

  • horizons (sequence of float, str or Quantity) – how far ahead to predict (plain numbers are ms)

  • step (float, str or Quantity) – time between the starting points of the forecasts (default 1 s)

Return type:

pandas.DataFrame

Returns:

one row per eye and horizon: horizon (s), rmse (root mean squared error, log-%), z_sd (standard deviation of the standardised errors) and n (number of forecasts)

interpolate(data=None, eyes=[], store_as=None, margin=None, inplace=None)[source]#

Replace the unusable samples of the whole recording (blinks, artifacts, signal losses of any length) by the expected signal under the model.

The samples that are unusable under the mask policy of the model, extended by margin, are replaced by predict("signal"): the expected pupil size given all usable data of the segment. Other than the interpolation of single blinks (d.pupil.blinks_interpolate()), this uses one model for the whole recording, which also bridges long gaps (with the slow components). The replaced samples get the "interpolated" layer. Samples outside the segments of the model (e.g., excluded periods) are not changed.

Parameters:

  • data (EyeData, optional) – object to interpolate; default: the data the model was built from. It must have the same samples.

  • eyes (str or list) – eyes; if empty, all eyes of the model

  • store_as (str, optional) – variable the result is stored in; default: the variable of the model ("pupil"), which is replaced

  • margin (tuple, optional) – time before and after each run of unusable samples that is replaced too (plain numbers are ms); default: the margin of the model, so that exactly the samples that the model did not use are replaced

  • inplace (bool or None) – if True, change data in place and return it; if False, return a changed copy; if None, use the setting of the object

Return type:

EyeData

Returns:

the object with the interpolated data

predict(what='signal', eyes=[], cutoff='10 s', scale='original', interval=None, _samples=None)[source]#

Parts of the signal at the original samples.

The smoother runs at the sampling rate of the model; the model is defined in continuous time, so the posterior of the signal at the original samples follows exactly from the smoothed states (upsample()).

Parameters:

  • what (str or list of str) –

    • "signal" (default): the pupil size without the noise (all components), also where the data are missing

    • "slow": the mean and the atoms with a time scale of at least cutoff (the slowly changing level of the pupil)

    • "detrended": the signal minus its slow part

    • the name of a component ("mean", "fast" for the fast component alone, "3s", …, "diff") or a list of them

  • eyes (str or list) – eyes; if empty, all eyes of the model

  • cutoff (float, str or Quantity) – time scale from which on the atoms count as slow (plain numbers are ms; default 10 s)

  • scale ({"original", "log%"}) – "original" (default): in the units of the data. Parts with the mean (signal, slow) are median * exp(part / 100); parts without it are the difference between the signal and the signal without the part, so that slow + detrended = signal. "log%": on the scale of the model, \(100 \log(\text{pupil} / \text{median})\), where the parts add up.

  • interval (float, optional) – if given (e.g. 0.95), also return the limits of the pointwise posterior interval with this probability. On the original scale only for parts that contain the mean.

Return type:

dict

Returns:

eye -> array with one value per sample of the data (NaN outside the segments); with interval, eye -> (estimate, lower, upper)

simulate(duration=None, fs=None, seed=None, name=None)[source]#

Simulate a new recording from the fitted model.

The latent pupil size is drawn from the model with the estimated parameters (process()), scaled to the median pupil size of each eye (scale), and white noise with the estimated standard deviation is added.

Parameters:

  • duration (float, str or Quantity, optional) – duration (plain numbers are ms); default: the duration of the segments of the model

  • fs (float, optional) – sampling rate in Hz; default: the rate of the model. The noise of the model is that of block means at the rate of the model; at another rate, its standard deviation is scaled by \(\sqrt{f_s / f_{s,\text{model}}}\) (as for white noise of the raw samples; the noise of real trackers is not white).

  • seed (int, optional) – seed of the random numbers

  • name (str, optional) – name of the data (default: the name of the model with _sim)

Return type:

SimEyeData

Returns:

the simulated data, with the true components in d.truth (see TimescalePupil)

Examples

>>> d = pp.TimescaleModel(data).fit().simulate("10 min", seed=1)
>>> d.plot.plot_components()
