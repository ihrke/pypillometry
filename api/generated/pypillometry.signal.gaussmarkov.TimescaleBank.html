TimescaleBank#

class pypillometry.signal.gaussmarkov.TimescaleBank(timescales=(3000.0, 10000.0, 30000.0, 100000.0, 300000.0), nu=1.5, v0=0.25, channels=1, kappa=10000000.0)[source]#

Bases: object

Structure of a timescale-bank model (see bank) and the map between its parameter vector and state-space matrices.

The object holds no data and no parameter values. The parameter vector theta (see names) is unconstrained apart from box bounds (bounds()): logs of the standard deviations and of the length scale, and \(u_k = \log(v_k + v_0)\) for the atoms.

Parameters:

  • timescales (sequence of float) – time scales (length scales) of the atoms in ms, increasing; default 3, 10, 30, 100 and 300 s

  • nu (float) – smoothness of the atoms (1.5 (default) or 0.5, 2.5, …)

  • v0 (float) – offset of the variances of the atoms (signal units squared); atoms with a variance far below it are treated alike. Default 0.25, i.e. a standard deviation of 0.5, which suits signals in log-% (\(100 \log(x / \mathrm{median})\)).

  • channels (int or sequence of str) – 1 (default) or 2 channels, or their names (e.g. ("left", "right"))

  • kappa (float) – variance of the diffuse prior of the means

Examples

>>> bank = TimescaleBank()
>>> bank.names
['log_fast_sd', 'log_fast_ell', 'log_noise_sd', 'u_3s', 'u_10s', 'u_30s', 'u_100s', 'u_300s']
>>> theta = bank.theta(fast_sd=8., fast_ell=700., noise_sd=0.2, sd_30s=4.)
>>> {k: round(float(v), 3) for k, v in bank.params(theta).items()}
{'fast_sd': 8.0, 'fast_ell': 700.0, 'noise_sd': 0.2, 'sd_3s': 0.0, 'sd_10s': 0.0, 'sd_30s': 4.0, 'sd_100s': 0.0, 'sd_300s': 0.0}
>>> bank.lgssm(theta, fs=20).dim_state
13
>>> TimescaleBank(channels=("left", "right")).names[2:7]
['log_noise_sd_left', 'log_noise_sd_right', 'log_diff_sd', 'log_diff_ell', 'log_gain']

start(vg, vg_diff=None, floor=0.001)[source]#

Starting values from an empirical semivariogram.

The variogram of the model is linear in the variances of its components, so that for a dictionary of fast length scales and the atoms they follow from non-negative least squares on the relative errors; this cannot diverge. The variogram is used at about log-spaced lags (Variogram.at_log_lags()).

Parameters:

  • vg (Variogram) – of the signal (pooled within segments); with two channels, of their mean

  • vg_diff (Variogram, optional) – with two channels: of the difference of the channels

  • floor (float) – atoms that get no variance start at this fraction of the largest semivariance

Return type:

np.ndarray

Returns:

parameter vector within the bounds

upsample(theta, fs, sres, factor, weights, segments=None, n_out=None)[source]#

Posterior mean and variance of parts of the signal at a factor times higher sampling rate than the data the smoother was run on.

The model is defined in continuous time, so that the distribution of the state between two samples follows exactly from the smoothed states at these samples (a Gauss-Markov bridge). Sample t of the smoothed data is taken as the centre of the fine samples t * factor ... (t + 1) * factor - 1 (block means, see block_mean()).

Parameters:

  • theta (array) – parameter vector

  • fs (float) – sampling rate (Hz) of the data the smoother was run on

  • sres (SmootherResult) – from smooth()

  • factor (int) – number of fine samples per sample

  • weights (array, (dim,) or (q, dim)) – weights of the state for the part(s) of the signal (see select(); with two channels, pass theta to it)

  • segments (list of (start, stop), optional) – the segments used in smooth(); outside of them the result is NaN

  • n_out (int, optional) – number of fine samples (default T * factor; longer outputs are padded with NaN)

Returns:

mean, var (np.ndarray, (T * factor,) or (q, T * factor))
